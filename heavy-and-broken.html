Heavy and Broken

MEDIUM & MATERIALS
Cast Concrete, Shattered Mirror.
YEAR
2026
ROLE
Sculptor, Material Researcher
THEMATIC CORE
Fragility, Weight & Fractured Self-Perception

Concept & Thematic Exploration

Heavy and Broken is an investigation into the paradoxical nature of objects of vanity. Traditional handheld vanity mirrors are delicate, light, and ornamental — symbols of private intimacy and fragile beauty. By replacing delicate metal and glass with brutalist, cold architectural concrete, the object undergoes a violent transformation of weight and meaning.

The mirror becomes physically burdensome to hold. The fractures across its frame are not accidents; they are visible scars.

“An object designed to reflect beauty, made too heavy to hold and shattered into fragments.”

The Sculptural Process

The project began by taking a plastic impression molds of vintage baroque handheld mirrors. Coarse-aggregate concrete was mixed and hand-poured into the intricate cavities. Due to the extreme tension between the plastic mold and brittle cement, multiple castings fractured during curing.

Rather than discarding broken casts, I documented and incorporated the destruction. The four mirrors shown in the process progression reveal the journey: from complete breakage, through structural re-bonding, to the final insertion of sharp, angled mirror shards that scatter light in unpredictable directions.
