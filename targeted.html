The Brief & Concept

For this project, I worked on a brief by Graphic Matters, translating a pressing social issue into a gripping, immediately attention-grabbing visual concept for a poster. I wanted the final result to arrest the viewer's gaze instantly while communicating the profound seriousness and urgency of the subject matter.

The campaign challenges the viewer to look past superficial glamour and confront violence not as something distant or fictional, but as an unsettling, lived reality.

“This is not a horror movie, it's even more terrifying. This is real and it's happening now.”

Art Direction & Photoshoot Direction

I developed the concept, visual direction, and overall art direction myself. Taking on this directing role was exhilarating: I was responsible for styling, composition, poses, lighting, and visual atmosphere during the photoshoot all while being the model myself.

I contrasted high-society evening attire — pearl chandelier earrings, classic dark lipstick, tailored pin-stripes, and a cigarette — with cold, predatory physical dominance. The hands choking from behind become the central focal point, breaking through the monochrome stillness of the frame.

Visual Style & Vintage Horror Influence

For the visual language, I chose a dark, high-contrast aesthetic heavily influenced by vintage 1970s psychological horror movie posters and midnight cinema prints.

By combining black, dirty ivory white, and a deep saturated crimson red with coarse analog film grain and distressed print texture, I created an unsettling atmosphere. The contrast between glamorous imagery and disturbing subject matter is intentional: it makes the viewer look twice.

Reflections & Creative Growth

This project taught me how much I love working on the creative direction and production side of visual design — especially taking an initial raw conceptual sketch and orchestrating photography, styling, typography, and makeup into a complete, haunting visual world.
